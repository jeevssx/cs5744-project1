This section states what the Smart Campus Transportation System must do and the qualities it must have. Every requirement has an ID, and Sections 5, 6, and 7 use these IDs to show which part of the design meets each requirement.

4.1 Customer and Users

The customer is Virginia Tech, represented by its Parking Services office, which would fund and own the system. Blacksburg Transit, which the Town of Blacksburg operates, is the transit partner. Blacksburg Transit provides the vehicle and schedule data, and its staff use the operator dashboard.

Table 4-1. User classes.

IDUser classDescription and needs
U1StudentsThis class covers students who commute to campus or ride buses around campus. They need to know when the next bus will arrive and where to find an open lot that their permit allows.
U2Faculty and staffThis class covers employees who drive or ride to campus. They need to find an open lot near their building when they arrive and to check bus arrival times.
U3Visitors and event attendeesThis class covers people who come to campus for visits, games, and events. They need to find visitor and event parking and the buses that serve events.
U4Transit operatorsThis class covers Blacksburg Transit dispatchers and supervisors. They need to see where every bus is, spot problems early, and post alerts, detours, and trip cancellations.
U5Parking operatorsThis class covers Parking Services staff. They need to see every lot’s count, correct counts that drift, and close lots for events.
U6System administratorsThis class covers the staff who run the system. They need to load new schedules, keep lot data current, change settings, and manage staff accounts.

U1, U2, and U3 use the rider app on their phones or in a web browser. U4, U5, and U6 use the operator dashboard.

4.2 Scope
In scope
  • The system covers the Blacksburg Transit fixed routes that serve campus, including the Campus Shuttle and game-day service.
  • The system covers the Virginia Tech parking lots and garages on the Blacksburg campus.
  • The system provides a rider app for phones and web browsers, an operator dashboard, and alerts.
Out of scope
  • The system does not handle fare payment. Blacksburg Transit is fare-free.
  • The system does not sell parking permits or issue citations. The existing parking portal and the license-plate enforcement system handle these tasks.
  • The system does not book trips on BT Access, the paratransit service.
  • The system does not include buying or installing the location units on buses or the counting devices at lots.
4.3 Assumptions
  • A1. Each vehicle carries a location unit that reports through Blacksburg Transit’s vehicle system every 10 to 30 seconds. That system offers a data interface that the Smart Campus Transportation System can connect to. No public real-time feed for Blacksburg Transit was found.
  • A2. Each lot has counting devices at its entrances and exits. These devices are entry and exit counters or the license-plate cameras that Virginia Tech now uses for permits. Each device reports entry and exit events and sends a heartbeat at least every 60 seconds.
  • A3. Blacksburg Transit continues to publish its routes and schedules in GTFS format.
  • A4. Parking Services provides each lot’s capacity, allowed permit types, and status thresholds.
4.4 Terms

Table 4-2. Terms.

TermMeaning
VehicleA vehicle is a Blacksburg Transit bus or shuttle. A vehicle is in service while it runs a trip.
OperatorAn operator is a transit or parking staff member who uses the operator dashboard. Bus drivers are not called operators in this document.
GTFSGTFS (General Transit Feed Specification) is the standard file format for transit routes, stops, and schedules.
HeartbeatA heartbeat is a short message that a device sends regularly to show that it is working.
LiveA data source is live when it reports on time.
DelayedA data source is delayed when it has not reported for 90 seconds, or for 2 minutes in the case of a lot counting device.
SilentA data source is silent when it has not reported for 5 minutes, or for 2 minutes in the case of the whole vehicle data feed.
ScheduledA scheduled time comes from the timetable instead of from vehicle data.
EstimatedAn estimated lot status is a prediction based on how full the lot was at the same time in past weeks.
4.5 How to Read the Requirements

Functional requirements are numbered FR-1 to FR-13, and non-functional requirements are numbered NFR-1 to NFR-9. Must means the first release needs the requirement, and Should means the first release plans to include it. The last column says how a tester checks the requirement.

4.6 Functional Requirements

Table 4-3. Functional requirements.

IDFeatureRequirementPriorityHow it is checked
FR-1Vehicle trackingThe rider app shall show each in-service vehicle on a map with its route and the time of its last position report.MustDemonstration
FR-2Vehicle trackingIf a vehicle’s newest position is more than 90 seconds old, then the system shall label the vehicle and its arrival times “delayed”. After 5 minutes without a new position, the system shall remove the vehicle from the map and show scheduled times for its trips.MustTest
FR-3Vehicle trackingIf no vehicle reports at all for 2 minutes during service hours, then the system shall treat the whole vehicle data feed as silent, and the rider app shall show a notice that live locations are unavailable along with scheduled times.MustTest
FR-4Arrival timesFor a stop that the user selects, the rider app shall show the next three arrival times on each route, each labeled “live”, “delayed”, “scheduled”, or “canceled”.MustDemonstration
FR-5RoutesThe rider app shall show each route’s path, stops, and timetable, using the schedule that Blacksburg Transit publishes. Administrators shall be able to load a new schedule that takes effect on a set date.MustDemonstration
FR-6ParkingThe rider app shall show, for each lot, the number of open spaces, a status of “available”, “limited”, “full”, or “closed”, and the time of the last update. It shall also let users filter lots by permit type.MustDemonstration
FR-7ParkingIf a lot’s counting device sends nothing for 5 minutes, or the lot’s count drops below zero or goes above its capacity, then the system shall mark the live count “unavailable”, show an “estimated” status, and flag the lot on the operator dashboard.MustTest
FR-8Operator dashboardThe operator dashboard shall show how many minutes early or late each in-service vehicle is. It shall flag vehicles that are off route, more than 5 minutes late, delayed, or silent.MustTest
FR-9Operator dashboardThe operator dashboard shall show the vehicle data feed, each vehicle, and each lot counting device as “live”, “delayed”, or “silent”.MustDemonstration
FR-10Operator dashboardThe operator dashboard shall let transit operators create and end service alerts, detours, stop closures, and trip cancellations. The rider app shall show each one on the affected routes and stops while it is active.MustTest
FR-11Operator dashboardThe operator dashboard shall show every lot with its count and status. It shall let parking operators enter a manual count and close or reopen a lot.MustDemonstration
FR-12Operator dashboardOperators shall be able to start a saved event plan, such as a game-day plan, which applies its detours and its lot closures in one step.ShouldTest
FR-13NotificationsUsers shall be able to subscribe to routes, stops, and lots without signing in. The system shall send them a push notification within 1 minute when a subscribed route has a delay of more than 5 minutes or a canceled trip, or when a subscribed stop closes or a subscribed lot becomes full or closes.ShouldTest
4.7 Non-Functional Requirements

Table 4-4. Non-functional requirements.

IDQualityRequirementPriorityHow it is checked
NFR-1PerformanceA new vehicle position, arrival time, or lot count shall appear in the rider app within 30 seconds after the system receives it.MustTest
NFR-2ScalabilityThe system shall support 10,000 rider app users at the same time and answer 95% of their requests within 1 second. It shall also handle twice the current fleet without changes to its design.MustTest
NFR-3AvailabilityThe services that answer rider app requests shall be available for at least 99.5% of service hours each month.MustAnalysis
NFR-4ReliabilityIf one part of the system fails, then the rider app shall keep working and show the last known data with its time. The system shall not lose a message that it has accepted, and it shall set bad messages aside without stopping.MustTest
NFR-5Prediction accuracyLive arrival times shall score at least 80% on the ETA Accuracy Benchmark.ShouldAnalysis
NFR-6SecurityStaff shall sign in through the Virginia Tech Login Service with Duo and shall reach only the functions of their role. All connections shall be encrypted.MustTest
NFR-7PrivacyRiders shall be able to use the app without signing in. The system shall not receive or store license plate numbers.MustInspection
NFR-8AccessibilityThe rider app and the operator dashboard shall meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.1 at Level AA.MustInspection
NFR-9MaintainabilityChanging the vehicle data vendor or the type of lot counting device shall require changes only to the module that receives that data. Administrators shall be able to change the time limits in these requirements without changing code.MustAnalysis
